Swipe or fling vertically across this page to test touch scrolling. When Flinger Tracing is enabled, disappearing bubbles trace your finger across the screen.

1. Touch Event Dispatch & Compositor Scrolling

When a user places their finger on a touchscreen and initiates a swipe gesture, the operating system's touch driver samples contact coordinates at high frequency. In modern browsers, these raw hardware samples are delivered to the renderer process and exposed through touch event streams.

Because the touch listener on this page is registered with { passive: true } and removed completely when tracing is turned off, the browser's compositor thread never waits on JavaScript execution before initiating a scroll or fling gesture. Your scroll gestures remain hardware-accelerated and jank-free while the bubble trail renders on screen.

2. Fling Velocity & Momentum Decay

A fling gesture occurs when a user accelerates their finger across the viewport and lifts off while maintaining non-zero velocity. At the moment of touch release, the input system computes the release velocity vector by fitting a velocity tracker over recent historical touch samples.

If the magnitude of the release velocity exceeds the platform's minimum fling threshold, the browser transitions from direct finger-tracking scroll into momentum-driven fling scrolling. On Android and ChromeOS, this deceleration curve follows a physics friction model calibrated to mimic physical inertia.

3. Compositor Thread vs. Main Thread

One of the foundational architectural splits in modern browser engines is the separation between the Main Thread (where DOM layout, style recalculation, and JavaScript run) and the Compositor Thread (which receives raw input events from the browser process and composites tiled textures onto the GPU).

Because each tracing bubble uses pointer-events: none and fixed viewport coordinates, hit-testing passes straight through into the underlying scrollable document. The compositor updates the scroll offset immediately while forwarding passive input notifications.

4. Visualizing Gesture Trajectories

Inspecting touch and fling behavior visually is invaluable when diagnosing subtle input edge cases—such as diagonal drift during vertical flings, touch slop thresholds at gesture start, or velocity spikes near screen edges. A visual trail reveals the exact curvature and spacing of touch samples.

5. Scroll Anchoring & Boundary Overscroll

As a high-velocity fling approaches the top or bottom boundary of a scrollable container, the remaining kinetic energy is absorbed by an overscroll glow or elastic stretch effect. Throughout the entire gesture, active touch points continue firing movement updates as long as the finger remains in contact with the glass.
